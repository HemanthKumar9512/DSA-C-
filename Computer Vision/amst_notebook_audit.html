CRITICAL 5 Sparse Frequency Attention is a supervised Fisher criterion — misrepresented as an attention mechanism Cell 16 ▼
What it isSparseFrequencyAttention.fit() computes the Fisher Linear Discriminant ratio: for each band, it calculates between-class scatter (SB) divided by within-class scatter (SW), then takes softmax. This is a classic supervised filter-based feature selection method. It requires class labels y at fit time. A true attention mechanism (as cited — Vaswani et al. 2017) computes query-key-value interactions in descriptor space without direct class label supervision.
Why criticalThe novelty claim C4 states "a sparse frequency attention mechanism inspired by transformers." Any reviewer familiar with attention mechanisms or Fisher criterion will immediately recognise this misclassification. It also introduces a subtle data leakage risk: the Fisher weights are computed on the full dataset before CV splits in Cell 18, meaning test-set class information influences the weighting applied to test features.
Fix required

Either: (A) Rename it honestly to "Fisher-criterion band weighting" or "supervised spectral band selection" throughout code, paper, and figures — this is a legitimate contribution, just not an attention mechanism; or (B) Implement true attention: compute query/key vectors per sample in descriptor space and derive weights without direct class labels. Additionally, move the sfa.fit() call inside each CV fold using only training data to eliminate data leakage.

MAJOR 2 Occlusion model removes boundary points — not standard image occlusion Cell 32 ▼
What it isocc_cnt(cnt, frac) removes a sequential arc of boundary points (e.g. removing 30% of the contour chain starting at a random index). Standard occlusion experiments in the shape literature (Belongie 2002, Ling & Jacobs 2007) occlude the binary image by masking a spatial region (rectangle or sector), then re-extract the contour. Arc-removal and spatial-masking produce fundamentally different degradation patterns — arc-removal is not cited or validated in any benchmark.
Fix required

Implement standard image-level occlusion: apply a rectangular mask of area frac × img_area to the binary image (e.g., zero out the central square), then re-run the full contour extraction pipeline on the occluded binary. This matches established protocols and makes Figure 7 directly comparable to published results.
